Scholé vs Udemy Business

Udemy Business is a catalog of instructor videos. Scholé generates adaptive, interactive lessons from your own content. Here is how the two compare when the goal is a workforce that can do the job.

Try Scholé free Book a demo

The short version

What Udemy Business is

A large marketplace of instructor-created video courses, packaged for companies as Udemy Business with a curated catalog and analytics.

What Scholé is

An AI-native learning platform that builds adaptive, role-specific lessons from a company's own tools, documents, and workflows. Spun out of EPFL and UC Berkeley research, launched #1 on Product Hunt, and used by learners at Bank of America, NASA, Microsoft, Apple, NVIDIA, and Decathlon.

Pick Scholé if

You want training tied to your tools, your data, and your workflows, delivered in interactive formats that adapt per learner, rather than a video library people click through.

Where each one wins

Where Scholé wins

  • Interactive by default: quizzes with feedback, code checkers, role-plays, and hands-on checklists instead of passive video.
  • Content comes from your own documents, so it matches how your company actually works.
  • Adapts on the fly to each learner's answers and pace.
  • Consistent quality grounded in learning-science research rather than variable instructor quality.

Where Udemy Business is strong

  • Enormous breadth of topics at low cost per seat.
  • Fast access to trending technical topics from many instructors.
  • Familiar format for self-directed learners.

Common questions

Does Scholé have a course catalog like Udemy?

Scholé is not a catalog. It generates lessons from the material you provide and from job-relevant context, so the content is specific to your organization.

Is Scholé only for technical training?

No. It covers onboarding, compliance, product knowledge, sales enablement, and technical skills, using formats like role-plays and decision trees for non-technical topics.

Can learners still watch videos on Scholé?

Yes. Explainer video and audio are two of the 24 formats, used when they fit the concept.
